Start a 5S Cycle

Your first 5S cycle in five steps

Hover over any part of the 5S wheel to see what each S means and how to use it in your area.

Pick your workplace to reveal your steps

Sort — separate and remove what’s not needed
Set — place in sequence of use
Shine — clean to inspect
Standardize — countermeasure and reset to standard
Sustain — repeat and improve daily
01

Sort (Seiri)

Remove everything that is not needed for the work that happens in this area. When in doubt, red-tag it. A red tag is not a verdict — it is a question. The team decides within a set window, usually 48 hours.

Worked example A production cell red-tags unused fixtures and broken gauges. 30% of bench clutter leaves the area in 48 hours. The station is immediately easier to navigate and audit.
  • Walk the area and red-tag every item that is not needed for current work
  • Log each red-tagged item: description, location, reason, disposition date
  • Move or remove all tagged items within 48 hours and record the outcome
Open the Red Tag Template
02

Set (Seiton)

Assign a permanent home for every item that remains. Label locations (not just items) so it’s clear what belongs where and what’s missing. Anyone entering the area should be able to find what they need and return it where it belongs.

Worked example Shadow boards are installed at each station with labeled outlines for every hand tool. A new operator can find and return any tool without having to ask where it belongs.
  • Assign a labeled location to every item that stays in the area
  • Use the three-click rule in the office so anyone can find the file they need in three clicks or fewer.
  • Confirm a new team member could find and return any item without asking
03

Shine (Seiso)

Clean the area and inspect it as you clean. Shine is not housekeeping. It is a systematic inspection for leaks, wear, damage, and abnormalities that would otherwise hide until they escalate.

Worked example A daily 5-minute shine runs at shift start. During the first week, two coolant fittings are found dripping and a worn cable is flagged before it fails.
  • Define the daily shine routine: who, what, how long, what to look for
  • Schedule the shine on the shift board so it runs without prompting
  • Log any abnormalities found during shine and assign an owner for each
04

Standardize (Seiketsu)

Make the first three S visible and permanent with simple one-page visuals of the target condition so anyone can see what good looks like and check against it. Define how to correct abnormalities—who acts, how fast to return to normal, and when to escalate if the standard can’t be restored.

Worked example The cell lead builds a one-page 5S standard with before/after photos of the shadow board and clean bench. Posted at the area entry.
  • Build a one-page 5S standard with photos showing the target condition
  • Post the standard at the area where it can be checked during an audit walk
  • Confirm the team can assess the current state against the standard without coaching
Open the 5S Workstation Helper
05

Sustain (Shitsuke)

Repeat the first four S as part of everyday work, not just special events. A simple, scored audit on a regular cadence—posted on the board and watched for trends—makes abnormal conditions visible and keeps 5S from fading.

Worked example A weekly 5S audit runs every Friday. Scores post to the daily management board. A score drop triggers a same-day debrief at the board.
  • Set a recurring weekly audit on the team calendar before leaving this page
  • Assign the first auditor and rotate the role across the team each week
  • Post the audit score on the daily management board and debrief any drop same day

Set (Seiton)

Give everything a home, arranged in the order it is used and kept within easy reach at the point of use.

Worked example The team standardizes the shared drive folder structure and file naming convention. A new hire finds any prior-period report without asking.
  • Assign a labeled location to every item that stays in the area
  • Create a shadow board or location map showing the target arrangement
  • Confirm a new team member could find and return any item without asking

Shine (Seiso)

Clean the area and inspect it as you clean. Shine is not housekeeping. It is a systematic inspection for leaks, wear, damage, and abnormalities that would otherwise hide until they escalate.

Worked example A monthly digital shine runs the last Friday of each month: outdated documents are archived, broken links are fixed, and the desktop standard is checked.
  • Define the daily shine routine: who, what, how long, what to look for
  • Schedule the shine on the shift board so it runs without prompting
  • Log any abnormalities found during shine and assign an owner for each

  • Use naming conventions, version control, and shared templates for files.
  • Schedule time to cleanup and re-organize

Sustain (Shitsuke)

Audit on a cadence. A scored audit against the standard is the mechanism that keeps 5S from fading. For office areas a monthly walk is usually enough. The score goes on the board. Trends matter more than any single result.

Worked example A monthly 5S walk covers physical and digital: desk standard, shared drive structure, and filing convention. One improvement per cycle.

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 5S just cleaning? +

No. Cleaning is one of the five S’s — Shine — and even that is not primarily about cleanliness. Shine is a systematic inspection: cleaning is the method, finding abnormalities is the goal. The other four S’s define what belongs in an area, where it belongs, what the standard should look like, and how to sustain it. Together, they create a workplace where problems become visible before they escalate.

How is 5S different in an office? +

5S applies exactly the same way in an office setting, but the targets are different. Sort means archiving or deleting files and clearing desk clutter, not removing unused fixtures. Set means a consistent shared drive structure and naming convention, not shadow boards. Shine means a regular review of the digital workspace for outdated documents and broken links. The discipline is identical; the surface it runs on is information instead of tools and materials.

How often should we audit? +

Weekly for most production and lab areas. Monthly for office and shared service areas where the workspace changes more slowly. The cadence matters less than the consistency. A team that misses audits for two weeks will find that 5S has faded. Wingmate can set the schedule and send reminders so the audit runs without prompting.

What is a red tag? +

A red tag is a physical or digital label placed on any item whose need in the area is uncertain. It is a question, not a verdict. Once tagged, the team has a defined window — typically 48 hours — to decide: keep, relocate, return to stock, or remove. Everything that stays gets a permanent home. Everything that leaves is logged in the Red Tag Log so the decision is visible and reversible if needed.

How this works

  1. You start on a cluttered digital desktop, the kind most people actually work on.
  2. Work the five steps in order across four rounds: sort, set, shine, standardize, sustain.
  3. Each round scores what you kept, what you moved, and what you threw away, and Wingmate coaches you between rounds.
