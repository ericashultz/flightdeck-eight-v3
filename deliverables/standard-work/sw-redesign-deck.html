Standard Work · today

A definition page, not a tool.

It opens with a definition of Standard Work, then a grid of who / when / why cards.

In Action, Learning & Experiences and Associated Methods are empty placeholders.

Templates are a flat list of six downloads, with no guidance on which to use when.

Nothing on the page adapts to who is reading it.

FLIGHT DECK · Standard Work · current page
The findings

Four things weren’t working.

FINDING 01

No onramp

The number one adoption blocker. A brand-new employee lands with no place to start and no learning path.

FINDING 02

A content jungle

Volume without signal. Many templates and videos, no clear answer to which one fits the job in front of you.

FINDING 03

Templates without context

Over a hundred templates, no “what good looks like,” no most-used, no way to flag a broken one.

FINDING 04

Built for the floor

Examples assume a factory. Office and engineering teams have to translate every one by hand.

Five options

Five ways to fix it.

Each direction is a complete, working take on the same page, built against what the survey told us. The next five slides walk through each one, then open its live demo. Today we choose the one to build first.

A
Option A · answers “no onramp”

Start Here Onramp.

What it is
A guided first standard for someone brand new. Four moves, about twenty minutes, no prior Flight Deck knowledge assumed.
Why it looks this way
It opens with an “Is this you?” check so the right person self-selects, then walks them through numbered steps, checkboxes, and a progress bar. The page reads like a path, not a library.
Why it works
It answers the number one adoption blocker head on. A newcomer leaves with one completed standard and a reason to come back.

B
Option B · answers “built for the floor”

Function Switcher.

What it is
One Standard Work page that rewrites itself for your function. Tabs across the top switch every example, template, and bit of language between Manufacturing, Transactional, and Engineering.
Why it looks this way
The tabs sit where the eye lands first, so the page declares up front that it speaks your dialect. The layout underneath never moves, only the language changes, so nobody relearns the page to use it.
Why it works
It answers the loudest signal in the survey, 51 requests for non-floor examples, without splitting Standard Work into three pages to maintain.

C
Option C · answers “the content jungle”

Curated Kits.

What it is
Pre-built sets of templates and examples grouped by scenario, function, and role. A coach opens a kit and shares the whole thing by link.
Why it looks this way
A filter rail down the side cuts a hundred-plus templates to the handful that fit the job in front of you. Each kit is one card you copy and send, so the answer is a click, not a search.
Why it works
It keeps every piece of existing content but adds the curation layer that was missing. Coaches stop being the search engine.

D
Option D · answers “templates without context”

Example First.

What it is
The page leads with real worked examples from every function, office and floor alike, instead of a definition. A full Investor Relations close sits next to a shop-floor changeover.
Why it looks this way
A featured case study runs the whole story top to bottom, then a filterable gallery finds the example closest to your work. You see what good looks like before you ever see a blank template.
Why it works
People copy a good example far faster than they interpret a blank form. It turns the template library into a pattern library.

Option E · answers “findability”

Ask Wingmate.

What it is
A plain-language ask box. Type what you want to standardize and Wingmate returns the right template, a real example from your function, and the steps to apply it.
Why it looks this way
One input, front and center, the way people already expect to search. The answer comes back as three things at once, so there is nothing left to go find.
Why it works
It removes naming and navigation from the problem entirely. You no longer have to know what the tool is called to find it.
