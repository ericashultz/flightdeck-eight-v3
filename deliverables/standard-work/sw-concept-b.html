The four moves

Your first standard in four steps

Each step has a short action, a worked example, the template it points to, a time estimate, and a small checklist. Check items off as you go. The progress bar at the top tracks your total.

01

Define the task

Before anything is written down, the task needs a name and a single best known way to do it today. Pick one discrete piece of work with a clear start and end. Resist the urge to capture a whole process at once.

Worked example A quality engineer at an engine assembly cell wants to document the torque check sequence before sign-off. The task is "Verify torque on bolts 1 through 8 prior to final close." One task, one owner, current best method only.
5 minutes Starter Worksheet · available in Kit below
  • Write the task name in one sentence (verb first, e.g. "Inspect," "Submit," "Verify")
  • Name the single owner for this task right now
  • Confirm this is what actually happens today, not the ideal version
Open the Starter Worksheet
02

Document it

Choose the right template for your type of work, then capture the sequence, timing, and any key points that matter for quality or safety. Aim for a draft, not a final. You will improve it later.

Worked example The same engineer opens the Manufacturing Standard Work template. She lists each step in order, fills in the time for each, and flags two steps that have a safety note. Total time in the template: 8 minutes. She does not wait for it to be perfect.
8 minutes Manufacturing SW · Transactional SW · Leader SW
  • Pick the template that matches your work type (manufacturing, transactional, or leader)
  • List every step in the real order they happen, including hand-offs
  • Note the target time and any safety or quality critical points
Pick your template
03

Visualize it

Post the standard where the work actually happens. For floor teams, that is the workstation. For office or engineering teams, that is the shared drive, the team page, or a printed sheet in the meeting room. The test: can someone find it without asking?

Worked example The engineer exports a one-page PDF and tapes it to the tool board next to the torque wrench. She also uploads it to the team SharePoint folder and links it from the cell's daily management board. Two placements, zero extra tools.
3 minutes Visual posting guide · Templates library
  • Post or share the standard in the place where the work happens
  • Confirm at least one colleague can locate it without asking you
  • Link it from the team's daily management board or equivalent
See visual posting examples
04

Sustain it

A standard is only useful if it stays current. Set a review date before you leave this page. The cadence does not need to be complex. Monthly for most transactional work. After every process change for manufacturing.

Worked example The engineer puts a recurring calendar event for the last Friday of every month labeled "Review torque standard." When she trains a new person, she walks through the standard with them instead of explaining from memory. After three months the standard has been updated twice.
2 minutes to set up Leader Standard Work template · Review cadence guide
  • Set a recurring review date before closing this tab
  • Identify the person responsible for keeping it current (can be you)
  • Use it the next time you onboard or coach someone on this task

Common questions

Common questions

My work is not repetitive. Does standard work apply? +

Yes. Standard work applies to any task that repeats at least once. Even knowledge work has sequences: how you structure a report, how you run a design review, how you hand off a drawing. You are not locking in a rigid script. You are capturing the best known approach so you have a baseline to improve from. The standard changes when you find a better way.

How detailed does the standard need to be? +

Detailed enough that someone new could follow it on day one without asking questions. That is the test. Most first drafts are too long. Aim for the steps that actually affect quality, safety, or timing. Everything else can live in a supporting note or link.

What is the difference between Manufacturing SW, Transactional SW, and Leader SW? +

Manufacturing Standard Work captures physical steps at a workstation with cycle times and takt time. Transactional Standard Work captures sequences for office, service, or engineering tasks where the work is information-based, not physical. Leader Standard Work captures the daily and weekly routines a team leader follows to observe, develop, and sustain a system. If you are not on the shop floor, start with Transactional.

How often should I update a standard? +

Update it whenever the process changes, whenever you find a better way, or whenever training someone reveals a gap. For stable processes, a monthly review is enough. A standard that has not been reviewed in six months is almost certainly out of date.
