Manufacturing · Cell Daily Management

Engine Assembly Line

How a production cell sequences, times and displays the work so every operator knows the right method, in the right order, at the right time.

Worked Example
CF6 Final Assembly Cell, Lynn, MA
A four-station cell with 12 operators building to a 2.4-hour takt. Standard work is posted at each station and audited daily by the cell lead.
Process Steps
  1. Define the takt time from customer demand and available production hours.
  2. Sequence each task in the order operators must complete it; assign time values.
  3. Identify the right-sized standard work in process inventory at each station.
  4. Post the standard work sheet visually at the point of use, not in a binder.
  5. Train operators to the standard; document training completion.
  6. Audit adherence daily via leader standard work observation sheet.
  7. Update the standard within 24 hours of any confirmed method change; retire the old version.
Template
Manufacturing Standard Work Sheet
Captures operator sequence, times, SWIP and safety points in a single-page format readable at a glance from the line.

What Good Looks Like
Visible at the line, not filed away
Time-balanced to takt across all stations
Updated version-controlled with a date
Operators can explain the standard in their own words
Who Owns It

Cell lead with manufacturing engineer support. Not the process engineer working remotely.

Common Pitfall

Documenting the standard once and never refreshing it after a process change. Stale standards undermine the whole system within weeks.

Transactional · Office and Services

Quarterly Earnings Close · S&OP Process

How office teams eliminate variation in high-stakes, repeating cycles so the outcome is predictable regardless of who is in the room.

Worked Example
Investor Relations · Quarterly Earnings Close
A 28-day close cycle involving Finance, Legal, IR, Communications, and the Executive team. Standard work reduces the cycle from 34 days to 26 days and eliminates four recurring late-night escalations.
Process Steps
  1. Map every hand-off in the current close cycle; identify who receives what and by when.
  2. Assign a named owner to each step, not a team or a role title.
  3. Define the standard for each deliverable: format, completeness criteria, sign-off required.
  4. Set the cadence: which steps happen daily, weekly, at each milestone.
  5. Build an escalation path for when a step is missed; do not rely on heroics.
  6. Run the first cycle to the new standard; capture actual times vs. planned.
  7. Close the loop at a 30-minute debrief: what held, what slipped, one update to the standard.
Second Example
S&OP Process US · Monthly Demand Review
A 5-day demand review sub-cycle feeding the main S&OP gate. Standard work defines data cutoffs, review owners, and the single agreed output entering the supply review.

What Good Looks Like
Any trained team member can run the cycle
Variation narrows over successive cycles
Escalation is defined before the crisis, not during it

Who Owns It

Process lead for the cycle. For earnings close: CFO Chief of Staff. For S&OP: Demand Planning Manager.

Common Pitfall

Treating a shared calendar as standard work. Calendar shows when things happen. Standard work shows how, by whom, to what standard, and with what escalation path.

Engineering · Design and Analysis

Engine Shop Visit (METCIS) · Field Problem Resolution

How engineering teams capture a repeatable method so knowledge transfers across engineers, programs and sites without starting from scratch each time.

Worked Example
METCIS · Engine Shop Visit Inspection Standard
Inspection workflow for a GEnx shop visit: 22 steps, 4 decision gates, owner assigned at each gate. Standard work is the method. The engineering spec is the requirement. They are different documents.
Process Steps
  1. Identify the engineering task type: analysis, design review, field investigation, or inspection.
  2. Define the method, not just the output. Capture the sequence an expert uses.
  3. Identify the tools required at each step (software, checklist, instrument) and specify the version.
  4. Define the decision gates: what stops the work, what constitutes a pass, who approves.
  5. Capture the common failure modes and the correct response for each.
  6. Validate the standard with two engineers who did not write it; resolve gaps.
  7. Store the standard where the work is done, not in a project folder no one finds.
Second Example
Field Problem Resolution
Standard work for field investigation sequences: first data pull, containment call, root cause analysis, and corrective action routing. Ensures any engineer on call reaches the same decision points in the same order.

What Good Looks Like
A new engineer produces the same result as a 10-year veteran
The method, not just the output spec, is documented

Who Owns It

Lead engineer for the task type. For shop visit standards: Chief Inspection Engineer. For field investigations: Field Engineering Manager.

Common Pitfall

Confusing the technical specification (what the output must be) with standard work (how to produce it). Both are required. They are not the same document.

How to use these templates

Step 1 · Choose

Select the template family that matches your function: Manufacturing, Transactional, Engineering, or Leader. Do not force a manufacturing template onto an office process.

Step 2 · Draft

Use the worked example for your function as a reference. Fill in your own steps, owners, and time standards. The first draft does not have to be perfect. Get it on paper.

Step 3 · Validate

Walk through the draft with one person who did not write it. Find the gaps. Update and post it where the work happens. Review it after the first cycle.
