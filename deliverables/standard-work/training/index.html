Standard Work Documentation

The six standard work templates, taught in build order, then proven on the floor at GE Aerospace Greenville. Twenty one minutes from blank form to a deployed, sustained standard.

The 2 minute take

Standard work is how a manufacturing process becomes visible, repeatable, and improvable. This training walks through the six templates that build it, in the exact order you would use them on the floor.

You start with the Time Measurement Form: watch at least ten cycles, mark a clean breakpoint between each work element, and use the lowest repeatable time, not the lucky fast one, to find the real cycle time. The Machine Capacity Sheet separates the operator's hands on time from auto run time and asks the only question that matters: can this machine meet demand?

The Balance Chart stacks each operator's load against the takt line. Under the line is capacity, the gap is waste, over the line is a problem. The Standard Work Combination Sheet draws one cycle as solid (manual), wavy (walk), and dotted (machine) lines against a takt line, so you see instantly whether a cycle finishes on time. The Process Scroll maps material and operator flow to expose hard and soft bottlenecks, and the Standard Worksheet rolls it all into the single sheet operators actually run, with safety, quality, and sign off built in.

Then Melvin McKinney shows it live at Greenville, where they machine turbine blades for CFM, Boeing, Airbus, and Honda. Standard work caught an ergonomic hazard, surfaced quality issues, and gave the operator a simple test: am I getting a part off every four minutes for the next process? When the answer slips, the standard makes it obvious, and the team problem solves back to standard. That is the whole point: not fewer people, but a safe, quality product delivered on takt.

The six templates

Each answers one question. Built in sequence, they turn an observed process into a documented standard.

01

Time Measurement Form

List work elements with breakpoints, observe 10+ cycles, and use the lowest repeatable time plus an adjustment to find the true cycle time.

02

Machine Capacity Sheet

Split manual time from auto run time for every machine, then compare time per item to actual excess capacity against demand.

03

Balance Chart

One bar per operator under a takt line. Under = capacity, the gap = waste, over = beyond takt and demand cannot be met.

04

Standard Work Combination Sheet

Steps in order with manual (solid), walk (wavy), and machine (dotted) time against a takt line. Does one cycle finish in takt?

05

Process Scroll

A layout of material and operator flow that reveals standard WIP, staffing plays, and hard vs soft bottlenecks.

06

Standard Worksheet

The single sheet that combines everything: layout, work elements, non cyclical work, and safety, quality, owner sign off.
