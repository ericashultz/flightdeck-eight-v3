How Investor Relations standardized the earnings close

A transactional team, no factory floor in sight, used Standard Work to turn a stressful quarterly scramble into a documented, repeatable close. Start from their standard, then make it yours.

1standard, end to end
4moves to copy it
The situation before
Before

A quarterly scramble

Every close felt like the first one. Tribal knowledge lived in email threads and individual heroics, not a shared method anyone could follow or improve.

  • No single owner for each step of the close sequence
  • Deadline awareness varied by person, not by role
  • Rework rate spiked in the final 48 hours before earnings
  • New team members had no written reference to onboard against
After

A documented, repeatable close

The team built one end-to-end standard. Every step is owned, sequenced, and reviewed. The close now runs the same way whether the original authors are in the room or not.

  • Each step has a named owner and a clear time box
  • New members onboard in a single session using the standard
  • Review cycle catches exceptions 3 days earlier on average
  • The standard is the first thing the team opens on close day
The 4 moves they used
Move 1

Define the sequence

Map every step from close trigger to published earnings, in the order it actually happens. No gaps, no assumed handoffs.

Move 2

Document to the step

Write each step as a verb plus an output: who does it, what they produce, and how long it should take at steady state.

Move 3

Visualize the flow

Build a one-page timeline view that shows the full close at a glance so anyone can spot if the sequence is on track or drifting.

Move 4

Sustain with a review cycle

After each close, spend 20 minutes updating the standard. What changed, what broke, what got faster. The document improves every quarter.
