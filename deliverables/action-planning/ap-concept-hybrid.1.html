Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can two people own an action? +

No. Shared ownership usually produces weak ownership. When two people share one action, each can assume the other is carrying it. Name the one person who will be asked at the next review if the action is complete. That person is the owner. If the work truly requires two people, divide it into two actions: one owner per action, each with its own deliverable and due date.

How often should we review the plan? +

Match the review cadence to the speed and risk of the work, but never let the plan sit unmanaged. Daily stand-up actions should be reviewed in the next stand-up. Longer follow-through actions should be reviewed at least weekly. Action plans are living documents that should be reviewed at least once a week. If slippage can go unnoticed for days, the cadence is too slow.

What makes a good action? +

A good action has three qualities. First, it is specific enough that the team knows exactly what will be delivered. Second, it has one named owner and a real date, not a vague period like a month or quarter. Third, it connects to impact, not just activity, with a result the team can verify. If those elements are missing, the action needs more definition before it belongs on the plan.

How is this different from a project plan? +

A project plan lays out milestones, dependencies, and timing across a longer horizon. An action plan captures the specific, owned, time-bound actions that move one KPI or target outcome. Many project plans require action planning inside key milestones. But Action Planning is not just project tracking - it is the discipline that connects daily and weekly execution to measurable KPI impact. Use it when the goal is to close a gap, not just to schedule work.
