Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

When do I use a PSR vs just fixing it? +

Fix it directly when the cause is known and the fix is immediate and within your control. Use a Problem Solving Report (PSR) when the problem recurs, the cause is unclear, or the fix requires coordination across people, shifts, or functions.

What if I cannot find the root cause? +

First, check that your problem statement is specific enough. Vague problems produce vague causes. If the statement is sharp and you are still stuck, try switching methods: move from 5 Whys to a fishbone diagram to expose categories of causes you have not explored. Use a coach, teammate, or Wingmate to challenge each "why" answer you have written so far. If the cause genuinely requires data across multiple cycles or sites to confirm, that is a signal to escalate beyond the local team.

How is this different from a corrective action? +

A corrective action in a quality management system (CAR, CAPA) is a formal record of a fix required by a customer, audit, or regulatory requirement. A Problem Solving Report (PSR) is a thinking tool you use to reach the right fix before you document it. The PSR informs the corrective action; it is not a substitute for it. In practice, the PSR often becomes the evidence package that supports the CAR.

Who should be in the room? +

For At Point Problem Solving (APPS): the person closest to the work, supported by a coach or team leader as needed, no large meeting required. For a Structured PSR: the process owner and one or two people who see the problem directly (Voice of the Process), ideally from different shifts or roles. Avoid large groups at the root-cause step. For a Kaizen event: a defined cross-functional team with a sponsor, a lean coach, and representatives from each affected area, together at genba. The right people are those who can observe the problem and implement the countermeasure, not those who need to be informed of it.
