User Journeys

The biggest takeaway from discovery: we are solving for user journeys, not personas. The board reads in three layers. Keep the three personas as shorthand, but design the experience around intent — what someone is trying to do.

The reframe
Design for intent, not identity.
Personas tell us who is in the room. Intent tells us what they came to do. The experience should meet the intent the moment someone lands.
Layer 1
What they're learning
Standard Work, Problem Solving, 5S, Value Stream Mapping, and more.
Layer 2
Who they are
Plant Leaders, FLIGHT DECK Leaders, Frontline Leaders, New Users.
Layer 3
Where they are in maturity
New, Learning, Applying, Coaching, Leading.

Keep the three personas

A shorthand for the room, not the thing we design against.

Nate
The NewcomerNew to the system. Needs an onramp and the daily basics.
Maggie
The Multiplier · CoachCoaches others and scales good practice across the floor.
Sam
The Standardizer · LeaderStandardizes the work and leads transformation.

Design around intent

Five intents the experience answers, whoever the person is.

01
I'm newStart here. A guided onramp to the basics.
02
I want to learnA clear path through Standard Work, Problem Solving, 5S, and beyond.
03
I need a templateFind the right template fast, then apply it without leaving the page.
04
I'm coaching othersCoaching kits, shareable Standard Work, and the Huddle Coach.
05
I'm leading transformationThe view across maturity, with the tools to move people up it.

The Foundry

The monthly routine that keeps the hub getting better instead of slowly getting worse. Feedback comes in, gets sorted and owned, and turns into fixes you can point to.

What it is, in plain terms

A redesign is great on launch day, but new content and requests pile up until the hub is a cluttered mess again. The Foundry stops that. Every month GE's user-feedback survey comes in and runs through seven stations: each real comment gets filtered, labeled, given one owner, sorted into one of four queues, acted on against a deadline, and counted so you can see whether problems are growing or shrinking.

The AI suggests how to sort a comment; a person makes the final call. Every comment gets exactly one owner. Angry or blocked users go first. Bttr. builds the system during the engagement, then hands GE the keys to run it forever.

In one line: it turns "people are complaining" into "here's exactly what we fixed."

Action queues

where Station 05 routes
  • Acknowledge: close the loop with the user. SLA: same week.
  • Fix: content / template / hub change. Goes to the GE owner.
  • Escalate: blockers like SSO. Routes to Risks tab.
  • Monitor: single mention or low signal. Watch for repeat.

Decision rules · the floor manager guidelines

No new themes Don't invent a 15th theme. Force every comment into the locked taxonomy. Only add a cluster when 3+ comments don't fit and the pattern repeats across two batches.
AI assists Wingmate suggests, human confirms. Sam's call from May 4 holds: AI categorization alone made the work harder. The right line is suggestion-with-override, not auto-classify.
Single owner Every comment has exactly one owner. Multiple owners = no owner. If two functions touch it, the assigner picks the most accountable one and CCs the other.
Negatives forward Pull negatives to the top of the batch. GE's pattern from the April cycle. The factory inherits this: blockers and negative-sentiment comments process first.
Privacy SSO is opt-in only. Survey is anonymous unless the respondent voluntarily opts in for follow-up. DT data control plan governs — we never join SSO without consent.
Trend honestly Don't recycle population yet. Each month surveys fresh visitors. Same-population resurvey is a future step. Until then, trends measure cumulative drift, not user-level change.

How Miro shows the factory in action

  • Layout · seven stations as Miro frames left to right. Each frame holds the in-flight comments at that stage.
  • Stickies move forward · a comment is a sticky note. As it gets labeled and routed, it slides through the pipeline. The board is the live state.
  • Reference shelf above · the taxonomy, tag dictionary, and queues sit at the top, always visible. Drag-from-shelf to apply.
  • Workshop sessions · the GE team runs a batch live. Bttr. observes during Phase 1, helps codify the rules, and hands back the running floor at Phase 2 handoff.
  • Bttr's role · design the factory, make Wingmate-assist work at Station 03, then hand off. GE runs it forever after.

Why this matters · the redesign compounds, not decays

After Sept 4, the operating loop runs forever
Without a triage loop, any redesign is a snapshot — sharp on day one, slowly broadening over time as new templates and new requests pile in. With the factory running, every month's comments either confirm the design holds or surface what needs adjusting. The redesign compounds — and the GE team owns the loop after handoff.
